Project Context

As Data Protection Delegate (DPD), I conducted a full Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA/EIPD) under GDPR Article 35 for the Hospital General de Gotan, a fictitious healthcare organization migrating its clinical management system to a cloud environment.

The assessment addressed the high-risk processing of sensitive health data (special category data under GDPR Article 9) during and after migration, ensuring that privacy-by-design and privacy-by-default principles were embedded in the target architecture.

GDPR Art. 35 ISO 31000 ISO 27001 RGPD Art. 9 Privacy by Design

Methodology

The DPIA followed a structured risk-based methodology aligned with ISO 31000 risk management principles and GDPR supervisory authority guidance:

Phase 1: Systematic Description

Documented the data flows, processing operations, categories of data subjects (patients, clinical staff), types of personal data (health records, prescriptions, lab results), and the cloud infrastructure architecture supporting the new system.

Phase 2: Necessity & Proportionality Assessment

Evaluated whether the cloud migration was necessary and proportionate for the hospital’s operational objectives, examining data minimization, purpose limitation, and storage limitation compliance under GDPR principles.

Phase 3: Risk Identification & Assessment

Identified risks to the rights and freedoms of data subjects using a risk matrix (probability × impact), covering confidentiality, integrity, and availability of health data throughout the migration lifecycle.

Phase 4: Mitigation Measures

Defined technical and organizational measures to mitigate identified risks to acceptable residual levels, aligned with ISO 27001 Annex A controls.

Phase 5: DPD Consultation & Documentation

Prepared the formal DPD consultation report and maintained the DPIA register as required by GDPR accountability obligations.

Mitigation Controls Applied

Technical Controls

  • End-to-end encryption (AES-256) for data at rest and in transit
  • Multi-factor authentication (MFA) for all clinical system access
  • Role-based access control (RBAC) with least privilege for clinical staff
  • Data Loss Prevention (DLP) policies on health data classification
  • Cloud provider compliance verification (GDPR Art. 28 DPA requirements)
  • Backup and disaster recovery testing for continuity assurance

Organizational Controls

  • Data Protection Impact Assessment documentation and register
  • Staff awareness training on GDPR health data obligations
  • Incident response procedure aligned with GDPR 72-hour breach notification
  • Cloud provider audit rights and sub-processor governance
  • Privacy-by-design review in system architecture decisions
  • Periodic review cycle for DPIA reassessment
